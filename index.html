Grade with an AI tool without handing it your students' names

Drop in a set of papers. This tool replaces names, email addresses, student IDs and other personal details with placeholder tokens, and gives you a separate private file that maps those tokens back. You send only the placeholder version to the AI service. When the feedback comes back, this tool puts the real names into it.

Nothing you load here leaves your computer

All the work happens inside this browser tab. There is no server to upload to, no account, no analytics, and the page is served with a policy that blocks it from making network requests at all. How that is enforced, and what it does not cover.

How a batch works

  1. Roster in Paste your class list so the tool knows which names to look for.
  2. Papers in Word, PDF, Markdown or plain text. Metadata is read but never exported.
  3. You review Every match is shown in context. You confirm, reject, or redact more by hand.
  4. Two downloads The protected papers for the AI, and the private map for you only.
  5. Names come back Paste the AI's feedback in with your map to re-identify it.

What it can and cannot do

This tool assists with de-identification. It does not certify that a document is anonymous, and it cannot make a judgement about FERPA on your behalf.

The limits worth knowing before you start
  • It finds names from the roster you provide. Someone mentioned in an essay who is not on the roster — a parent, a roommate, a supervisor at a placement — will not be found automatically. You can redact those by hand on the review screen.
  • Removing names does not always make a paper anonymous. A student who writes that they are the only person from their country in the program, or describes a rare medical condition, may still be identifiable to a reader who knows the class.
  • Scanned PDFs have no text to read. Those are refused rather than passed through looking clean.

Check with your registrar, privacy office or IT department before using any AI service with student work. This tool is not legal advice, and its authors are not your institution.
